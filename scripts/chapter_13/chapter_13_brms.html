13 Models With Memory

Benefits of multilevel model:

  • better estimates for repeated sampling (9.e, in longitudinal data),
  • better estimates when there are imbalances among subsamples,
  • estimates of the variation across subsamples, and
  • avoiding simplistic averaging by retaining variation across subsamples.

Even though it seems like we can derive important insights from how the tank-level intercepts are distributed, that information is not explicitly encoded in the statistical model. Keep that in mind as we now consider the multilevel alternative. Its formula is \[ \begin{align} \text{surv}_i & \sim \text{Binomial}(n_i, p_i) \\ \text{logit}(p_i) & = \alpha_{\text{tank}[i]} \\ \alpha_j & \sim \text{Normal}(\bar{\alpha}, \sigma) \\ \bar{\alpha} & \sim \text{Normal}(0, 1.5) \\ \sigma & \sim \text{Exponential}(1), \end{align} \]

13.2 Varying effects and the underfitting/overfitting trade-off

13.2.1 The model.

The simulation formula should look familiar \[ \begin{align} \text{surv}_{i} & \sim \text{Binomial}(n_i, p_i) \\ \text{logit}(p_i) & = \alpha_{\text{pond}[i]} \\ \alpha_j & \sim \text{Normal}(\bar{\alpha}, \sigma) \\ \bar{\alpha} & \sim \text{Normal}(0, 1.5) \\ \sigma & \sim \text{Exponential}(1) \end{align} \]

13.2.3 Simulate survivors

Each pond \(i\) has \(n_i\) potential survivors, and nature flips each tadpole’s coin, so to speak, with probability of survival \(p_i\). This probability \(p_i\) is implied by the model definition, and is equal to: \[ p_i = \frac{\text{exp}(\alpha_i)}{1 + \text{exp}(\alpha_i)} \] The model uses a logit link, and so the probability is defined by the [inv_logit_scaled()] function.

The initial multilevel update from model b11.4 follows the statistical formula \[ \begin{align} \text{left_pull}_i & \sim \text{Binomial}(n_i = 1, p_i) \\ \text{logit}(p_i) & = \alpha_{\text{actor}[i]} + \gamma_{\text{block}[i]} + \beta_{\text{treatment}[i]} \\ \beta_j & \sim \text{Normal}(0, 0.5) \hspace{1em} ,\text{for} j = 1, ..., 4 \\ \alpha_j & \sim \text{Normal}(\bar{\alpha}, \sigma_\alpha) \hspace{1em} ,\text{for} j = 1, ..., 7 \\ \gamma_j & \sim \text{Normal}(0, \sigma_\gamma) \hspace{1em} ,\text{for} j = 1, ..., 6 \\ \bar{\alpha} & \sim \text{Normal}(0, 1.5) \\ \sigma_\alpha & \sim \text{Exponential}(1) \\ \sigma_\gamma & \sim \text{Exponential}(1). \end{align} \]

The statistical model using non-centered parameterization more faithful to the way it will be expressed with brms::brm():

\[ \begin{align} \text{left_pull} & \sim \text{Binomial}(n_i = 1, p_i) \\ \text{logit}(p_i) & = \bar{\alpha} + \beta_{\text{treatment}[i]} + z_{\text{actor}[i]}\sigma_\alpha + x_{\text{block}[i]}\sigma_\gamma \\ \bar{\alpha} & \sim \text{Normal}(0, 1.5) \\ \beta_j & \sim \text{Normal}(0, 0.5) \hspace{1em} ,\text{for} j = 1,...,4 \\ z_j & \sim \text{Normal}(0, 1) \\ x_j & \sim \text{Normal}(0, 1) \\ \sigma_\alpha & \sim \text{Exponential}(1) \\ \sigma_\gamma & \sim \text{Exponential}(1). \end{align} \]
